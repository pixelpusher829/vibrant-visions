Acrylics are the most forgiving way to start painting. They dry fast, clean up with water, and you can paint over a mistake within minutes. This guide takes you from a blank canvas to a finished small still life.

## 1. Set up your space

Work near a window if you can. Natural light shows colour more accurately than most bulbs. Lay down newspaper, fill **two** water jars (one for the first rinse, one for clean water), and squeeze out pea-sized amounts of each colour along the edge of your palette. Acrylics dry quickly, so put out a little at a time.

> **Tip:** A light mist from a spray bottle keeps paint on your palette workable for much longer.

## 2. Learn the paint

Before you paint anything real, fill a scrap sheet with tests:

- **Mix a colour wheel.** Make orange, green and violet from your primaries. Notice how a tiny touch of a strong colour (like blue) shifts a mix more than you expect.
- **Make a value scale.** Mix five steps from pure white to black. Value (lightness and darkness) does more to make a painting read well than colour does.
- **Try consistencies.** Paint a stroke straight from the tube, one thinned slightly with water, and a wash thinned a lot. Each has its own use.

## 3. Block in your composition

Set up a simple still life: one or two objects such as a mug and a piece of fruit, lit from one side. With a thinned neutral colour, sketch the main shapes directly onto the canvas. Keep it loose; this drawing will be covered.

Then use your large flat brush to block in the big areas of colour: background, table and objects. Don't worry about detail yet. Aim to cover the whole canvas so there's no white left showing.

## 4. Build up form

Once the first layer is touch-dry (usually 10–15 minutes), start modelling the objects:

1. Mix a **shadow** colour for each object, darker and slightly cooler than its base colour.
2. Mix a **light** colour, lighter and slightly warmer.
3. Paint the shadow side, then the lit side, letting the base colour show in between as a mid-tone.
4. Soften edges where light turns into shadow by blending while the paint is still wet.

## 5. Finish with details

Switch to your small brush for highlights, the edge of the table and any crisp lines. Less is more here. A single bright highlight on a piece of fruit can bring the whole painting to life.

## Common beginner mistakes

- **Overworking.** If the colours are turning muddy, let the layer dry and come back to it.
- **Using too little paint.** Acrylic darkens slightly as it dries. Lay it on generously.
- **Letting brushes dry with paint in them.** Dried acrylic ruins brushes. Keep them in water while you work and wash them with soap when you're done.

## Keep going

Paint the same setup three times in a week, changing only the lighting. You'll learn more from that than from three different subjects. When you're ready for feedback, bring your canvases along to an open studio night or a critique circle.
